**Life is poker, not chess.

You must make decisions with incomplete information, influenced by luck and uncontrollable factors (like your starting "hand"), rather than having perfect knowledge and equal footing like in chess, emphasizing skill in adapting to uncertainty and managing risk, not just finding the single "right" move.

In chess, skill dominates. In poker (and life), a great decision can still lose due to bad luck, and a poor decision can win by chance. This highlights the importance of playing the hand you're dealt and choosing the right "table" (situation).

### The Core Idea

**Meaning:** You must make decisions with incomplete information, influenced by luck and uncontrollable factors (like your starting "hand"), rather than having perfect knowledge and equal footing like in chess. This emphasizes skill in adapting to uncertainty and managing risk, not just finding the single "right" move.

### Why It Matters

In chess:
- Skill dominates outcomes
- Perfect information available
- One "best" move exists
- Equal starting position

In poker (and life):
- Great decision can still lose due to bad luck
- Poor decision can win by chance
- Starting hand matters (you don't control it)
- Skill is managing what you CAN control given what you HAVE

### The Lesson

**Play the hand you're dealt.** Choose the right table (situation/environment). Build skills in:
- Risk assessment
- Uncertainty management
- Adaptation
- Long-term thinking over single outcomes

Success isn't about always being "right"—it's about making good decisions repeatedly, understanding some wins and losses are beyond your control.
